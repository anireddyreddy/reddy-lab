Research statement

Overview

Plants are sessile and must continually sense and cope with changes in their environment. Our laboratory investigates two layers of regulation that link perception to response: calcium/calmodulin-mediated signal transduction and post-transcriptional control of gene expression through pre-mRNA splicing and the epitranscriptome.

We combine genetics, biochemistry and cell biology with single-molecule long-read sequencing and machine learning to map transcriptome complexity, understand regulatory genomics across model and cereal crop species, to identify the cis-elements and trans-acting factors that govern splicing, and to define how stimulus-specific Ca²⁺ signatures are decoded by calmodulin and its targets to reprogram gene expression.

Theme 1 · Calcium signaling

Decoding Ca²⁺ signatures through calmodulin and its targets

We identified calmodulin-binding transcription factors (CAMTAs/AtSRs), kinesins and metabolic enzymes that connect Ca²⁺ transients to growth, immunity and abiotic stress responses, including Ca²⁺/calmodulin control of salicylic-acid-mediated immunity.

  • Calmodulin-binding proteins and CAMTA/SR transcription factors
  • Ca²⁺-regulated gene expression under biotic and abiotic stress

Theme 2 · Pre-mRNA splicing

Alternative splicing and the plant splicing code

We study serine/arginine-rich (SR) and SR-like proteins such as SR45, their autoregulation and interactions with the spliceosome, and we use long-read sequencing to reveal the extent and stress responsiveness of alternative splicing in Arabidopsis, sorghum and other crops.

  • SR proteins, splicing regulatory elements and the splicing code
  • Co-transcriptional splicing and nuclear speckle dynamics
  • Isoform-resolved transcriptomes of crops
Splicing papers →
Theme 3 · Epitranscriptome

The plant epitranscriptome

Using nanopore direct RNA sequencing, we profile N⁶-methyladenosine (m⁶A) modifications together with splicing, polyadenylation and transcription start sites on full-length molecules, to understand how modifications coordinate RNA processing.

  • m⁶A writers, readers and erasers in plants
  • Crosstalk between the epitranscriptome and splicing
Epitranscriptome papers →
Theme 4 · Computational genomics

Machine learning for regulatory genomics

In long-standing collaboration with computer scientists, including Asa Ben-Hur (Department of Computer Science, Colorado State University), Lianfeng Gu (Fujian Agriculture and Forestry University, China) and Runxuan Zhang (James Hutton Institute, UK), we develop machine learning approaches, including deep learning tools, to predict gene regulation, splice sites, splicing regulatory elements, calmodulin-binding sites and epitranscriptomic marks from large nucleic acid sequence datasets.

  • Splicing code and isoform prediction
  • Long-read transcriptome assembly and annotation
Computational papers →
Theme 5 · Plant biotechnology

Engineering plants for agriculture and industry

We translate basic discoveries into tools and traits: betalain-based RUBY visual reporters that allow noninvasive monitoring of gene expression and splicing in living plants, metabolic engineering of Arabidopsis to produce platform chemicals such as phloroglucinol and butanetriol, disease-resistant transgenic potato and herbicide resistance through an auxin coreceptor mutation, and exploiting natural splicing variation for crop improvement.

  • RUBY visual reporters
  • Metabolic engineering for high-value products
  • Disease and herbicide resistance
